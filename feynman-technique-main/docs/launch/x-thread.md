To know the bird, watch what it does.

**6**

The famous test — "explain it to a 12-year-old" — he never said.

What he did say, 1966:

"Without using the new word which you have just learned, try to rephrase what you have just learned in your own language."

Cover the term. If the sentence dies, you wrote a definition.

**7**

My favourite thing he ever wrote is a rewrite. He opened a textbook at random:

"Triboluminescence is the light emitted when crystals are crushed."

His verdict: you've said what a word means in terms of other words. You've said nothing about nature.

---

**8**

He rewrote it:

"When you take a lump of sugar and crush it with a pair of pliers in the dark, you can see a bluish flash. Some other crystals do that too. Nobody knows why. The phenomenon is called 'triboluminescence.'"

Something you can do, then what you'd see, then the name.
